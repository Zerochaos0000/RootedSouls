The Reading and Writing section at a glance

54questions
64 mintwo 32-minute modules
~71 sper question
25–150words per passage, one question each

Every question has four choices, and there's no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave one blank. The test is adaptive: how you do in Module 1 decides whether Module 2 is easier or harder, and the harder module is where the highest scores come from. A few questions in each module are unscored trial questions, but you can't tell which ones, so treat them all the same.

What's tested

Within each module, questions come in the same order as below, grouped by type and usually running from easier to harder.

Craft and Structure

28%, 13–15 questions

Words in context, the purpose and structure of a text, and how two texts relate. Your Vocabulary and Word parts lists train this.

Information and Ideas

26%, 12–14 questions

Main ideas, supporting evidence (including tables and graphs), and logical conclusions.

Standard English Conventions

26%, 11–15 questions

Punctuation between clauses (Boundaries) and grammar like agreement, tense, and modifiers (Form, structure, and sense). Your Grammar list covers the most-tested rules.

Expression of Ideas

20%, 8–12 questions

Transitions and rhetorical synthesis (using notes to meet a goal). Your Transitions list trains the first.

Question types and how to approach them

Words in contextCraft and Structure

"Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?"

Cover the choices and say your own word for the blank, using the clues around it. Then pick the closest match. Common words often appear in a less common meaning, so check the Secondary meanings section.

Text structure and purposeCraft and Structure

"Which choice best describes the main purpose of the text?"

Summarize what the author is doing with a verb: introduces, challenges, explains, contrasts. Wrong choices usually describe only one sentence or go further than the text does.

Cross-text connectionsCraft and Structure

"Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to…?"

Before reading the choices, state each text's main claim and decide whether they agree, disagree, or partly agree.

Central ideas and detailsInformation and Ideas

"Which choice best states the main idea of the text?"

The main idea covers the whole passage. Eliminate choices that are true but only describe one detail.

Command of evidenceInformation and Ideas

"Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researcher's claim?" or "Which choice most effectively uses data from the table?"

Rewrite the claim in your own words, then pick the choice that matches every part of it. For graphs, check each choice against both the data and the claim; accurate data that doesn't support the claim is a common trap.

InferencesInformation and Ideas

"Which choice most logically completes the text?"

The answer must follow from what the text says, with no outside knowledge. Avoid choices that go a step further than the passage supports.

Boundaries and grammarStandard English Conventions

"Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?"

Find the subject and main verb first, then count how many complete sentences there are. Most punctuation answers follow from that.

TransitionsExpression of Ideas

"Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?"

Name the relationship between the two sentences (contrast, cause, example, addition) before looking at the choices. Don't pick by what sounds familiar.

Rhetorical synthesisExpression of Ideas

"The student wants to… Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?"

Read the goal first and ignore the notes until you need them. The right answer does exactly what the goal asks, even when other choices are also true.

Test-day habits

  • Answer every question. A guess can't cost you points.
  • Keep moving at about a minute per question. Flag hard ones and come back at the end of the module.
  • Take Module 1 seriously. It decides how high your score can go in Module 2.
  • Practice with the official full-length tests in the College Board's Bluebook app, the same app used on test day.
